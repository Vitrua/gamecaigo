The 10 questions, 4 answers each

1. Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta. Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta: which Venetian engineer designed the present church, built in 1899–1907?
A) Andrea Palladio  B) Pietro Saccardo  C) Baldassare Longhena  D) Giuseppe Jappelli

2. Villa Tiepolo Passi. Villa Tiepolo Passi: in which year was the villa’s chapel built, after a fire destroyed the earlier church?
A) 1344  B) 1710  C) 1899  D) 1774

3. Parco del Castello. Parco del Castello: the three towers on the coat of arms stand for the castles of Carbonera, Pezzan and which other village?
A) Vascon  B) Mignagola  C) Musestre  D) Varago

4. Drinking fountain Via Aurelio Bianchini. Drinking fountain Via Aurelio Bianchini: which ancient Roman road crosses the municipal territory?
A) Via Aemilia  B) Via Flaminia  C) Via Claudia Augusta Altinate  D) Via Appia

5. Park at Contrada di Bibano. Park at Contrada di Bibano: the first records of Carbonera date back to about which year?
A) 710  B) 1115  C) 1000  D) 1344

6. Chiesa di Pezzan. Chiesa di Pezzan: in which year was the name of San Filippo added to the dedication of the church, originally a chapel of San Giacomo?
A) 1344  B) 1115  C) 1710  D) 1774

7. Campo Giochi Don Cagnin. Campo Giochi Don Cagnin: how many Venetian villas of the municipality are protected by the Istituto Regionale Ville Venete?
A) Five  B) Eight  C) Twenty-one  D) Thirteen

8. Cimitero di Mignagola. Cimitero di Mignagola: in a parchment of 21 March 1121, a certain Oderico of Carbonera witnessed a donation to the church of which saint in Treviso?
A) San Nicolò  B) Santa Fosca  C) San Vito  D) San Teonisto

9. Church of San Giacomo di Musestre. Church of San Giacomo di Musestre: in 1115 Cluniac friars received a gift of the monastery dedicated to which apostle?
A) Saint Peter  B) Saint Paul  C) Saint James (San Giacomo)  D) Saint Andrew

10. Chiesa di Vascon. Chiesa di Vascon: in which year was the present coat of arms of Carbonera approved by municipal resolution no. 51?
A) 1929  B) 1967  C) 1945  D) 1966

Answer key (don’t scroll further until you’ve played): 1-B, 2-D, 3-A, 4-C, 5-C, 6-A, 7-D, 8-B, 9-C, 10-B

Sources and fun facts

1. Chiesa di Santa Maria Assunta: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Monumenti)
   Fun fact: An earlier church on the site dates back to about the year 1000.
2. Villa Tiepolo Passi: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Monumenti)
   Fun fact: Its frescoes are attributed to Giovan Battista Canal, a nephew of Canaletto.
3. Parco del Castello: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Storia, Simboli); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The tallest tower, in the centre, is Carbonera’s.
4. Drinking fountain Via Aurelio Bianchini: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Storia, Simboli); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The black field of the coat of arms recalls this road.
5. Park at Contrada di Bibano: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Storia); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The church of Carbonera belonged to the bishop’s estate of Treviso.
6. Chiesa di Pezzan: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Monumenti)
   Fun fact: A chapel of the Beata Vergine della Consolazione was added in 1710.
7. Campo Giochi Don Cagnin: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Monumenti); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: Villa Tiepolo Passi is the best known.
8. Cimitero di Mignagola: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Storia); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: He is one of the first people named as being from Carbonera.
9. Church of San Giacomo di Musestre: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Storia); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The gift was made to the Cluniac order.
10. Chiesa di Vascon: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonera (Simboli); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The prefect changed the first design on the advice of the State Archive of Treviso.
