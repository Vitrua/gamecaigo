Sources and fun facts

1. Mastio della Cittadella: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino and it.wikipedia Cittadella di Torino
   Fun fact: Only the Mastio, the entrance building, still stands; it houses the artillery museum.
2. Fontana Angelica: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (introduction); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The capital then moved to Florence.
3. Palazzo Madama: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Madama_(Torino)
   Fun fact: The building rises on the site of a Roman city gate, the Porta Praetoria.
4. Cattedrale di San Giovanni Battista: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin_Cathedral
   Fun fact: The cathedral was built between 1491 and 1498 on the site of the Roman theatre.
5. Porta Palatina: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Storia; Monumenti)
   Fun fact: The gate is one of the best-preserved Roman city gates in Europe.
6. Giardini Reali: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Storia); loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The Savoy residences are UNESCO World Heritage.
7. Piazza della Repubblica: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Mercati)
   Fun fact: Turin people call the market area Porta Pila in Piedmontese.
8. Santuario della Consolata: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santuario_della_Consolata
   Fun fact: Its Romanesque bell tower survives from the eleventh century.
9. Basilica di Maria Ausiliatrice: General knowledge (Salesians in Valdocco); Museo Casa Don Bosco and a Giovanni Bosco monument are mapped nearby in OSM; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The Salesian order began in this part of Turin.
10. Museo Diffuso della Resistenza: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Musei: Istituto Storico della Resistenza)
   Fun fact: It is run by the Resistance Historical Institute.
11. Museo Egizio: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Egizio
   Fun fact: It is the world’s oldest museum entirely dedicated to ancient Egypt.
12. Chiesa di Santa Cristina: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_San_Carlo_(Torino)
   Fun fact: Locals call it il salotto della città; it measures 168 by 76 metres.
13. Mole Antonelliana: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_Antonelliana
   Fun fact: It was begun as a synagogue and finished in 1889.
14. Chiesa di Santa Maria al Monte dei Cappuccini: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Musei: Museo nazionale della montagna)
   Fun fact: The church dates from 1583.
15. Chiesa della Gran Madre di Dio: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin
   Fun fact: Legend says the Holy Grail is hidden between its two statues.
16. Ponte Vittorio Emanuele I: General knowledge; Parco Michelotti description (it.wikipedia) places the bridge on the river; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The Po is Italy’s longest river.
17. Piazza Vittorio Veneto: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin (Piazza Vittorio and the Murazzi)
   Fun fact: The square is one of the city’s lively spots for bars.
18. Murazzi del Po: General knowledge; location from OSM way 106520984; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The Murazzi are the stone quays along the Po.
19. Ponte Umberto I: General knowledge; positions from OSM way 531546749 and way 549096612; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The bridge carries allegorical sculptural groups.
20. Parco del Valentino: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_del_Valentino
   Fun fact: Each lady called her partner “Valentino”.
21. Giardini Cavour: General knowledge (Cavour); Cavour monument mapped in OSM node 5415334293; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: He helped bring about Italian unification.
22. Piazza Carlo Felice fountain: General knowledge; positions from OSM way 561594672 and node 3395226356; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The square is the gateway to Via Roma.
23. Tempio Valdese: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_Antonelliana (Storia); it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Chiese)
   Fun fact: The Waldensian temple and the synagogue were built from the start for non-Catholic worship.
24. Castello del Valentino: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castello_del_Valentino
   Fun fact: It joined the UNESCO Savoy Residences site in 1997.
25. Borgo Medievale: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Architettura; Borgo Medievale)
   Fun fact: It copies Piedmontese and Aosta Valley castles and villages.
26. Centro Storico Fiat: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Storia, fine XIX secolo)
   Fun fact: Giovanni Agnelli founded it with partners.
27. Villa della Regina: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_della_Regina
   Fun fact: It was once a vineyard villa for Prince-Cardinal Maurice of Savoy.
28. Parco Michelotti: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_Michelotti
   Fun fact: The park lies between Ponte Vittorio Emanuele I and Ponte Regina Margherita.
29. Monument to Galileo Ferraris: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_Ferraris
   Fun fact: His work led to the alternating-current electric motor.
30. Monument to Vittorio Emanuele II: en.wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin
   Fun fact: The roundabout joins Corso Vittorio Emanuele II and Corso Galileo Ferraris.
31. Museo Pietro Micca: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Micca
   Fun fact: He was a soldier-miner of the Savoyard army.
32. Teatro Regio: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Regio_(Torino)
   Fun fact: Manon Lescaut also premiered here, in 1893; a fire destroyed the old theatre in 1936.
33. Museo di Antropologia Criminale Cesare Lombroso: it.wikipedia https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino (Musei); Lombroso’s field is general knowledge; loosely tied to the pin
   Fun fact: The museum is part of the university’s museum system.
